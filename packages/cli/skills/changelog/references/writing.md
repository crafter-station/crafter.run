# Writing standard

## Voice

- Plain and specific. Say what a person can now do, not how great it is.
- Present tense, active voice. "Pulse counts ships" beats "Ships are now being counted".
- Numbers only when you measured them and can link the source. Never round up.
- First person plural ("we") for team work, nothing grandiose. No "excited", "thrilled", "game-changing", "seamless", "unlock", "leverage".
- One idea per sentence. Short paragraphs. No rhetorical questions as openers.

## Deslop checklist (run on both languages before check)

- No em dashes. Use a comma, a colon or a period. `check` warns on them.
- No "not just X, but Y", no "no X, no Y, just Z", no triplet lists made for rhythm.
- No stacked adjectives, no "robust", "powerful", "cutting-edge".
- No emojis.
- Headings are plain words, not slogans.
- Spanish uses proper accents and natural phrasing, not a calque of the English.

## Facts

- Only from public sources: the live product, the public repo, merged PRs, published packages.
- Every claim has a link in `evidence` or in the text.
- Dates are the ship date, not the day you wrote the copy, unless they are the same.

## Never include

- Internal data: Slack or Notion content, internal hosts, private repos, unreleased plans, revenue, other companies' numbers.
- Names of alumni or people who are no longer on the core team page. `crafters` accepts only current core usernames.
- Benchmarks or comparisons against other named projects.
- Secrets, tokens, emails, screenshots showing private data. Check every frame.
